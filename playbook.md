```

David here. [Direct ask in first sentence — usually a meeting request]

Quick context: [One-sentence credibility — clients + Penn GSE]

[One-sentence proof point with a TRANSFORMATION STAT and a forward-looking
hook — "we're kicking off a new cohort..."]

[Bridge sentence connecting their work/POV to what follows]

1. [Topic word.] [One sentence with their language. One sentence with your differentiator.]

2. [Topic word.] [One sentence with their language. One sentence with your differentiator.]

Best from Atlanta,
David Fu
https://www.linkedin.com/in/davidthefu/
https://www.learningbydesign.ai/

P.S. [2-3 personal recognition points, numbered, each tied to a specific
fact about them — NEVER business in the P.S.]
```

**Word count target: 200-220 words. Hard cap at 250.**

## The 9 Rules

### 1. Lead with the question, not the hook
First line after "David here." is a clear, direct ask. "Would you be open to meeting at ASU GSV?" lands better than a clever hook. The hook comes *after* the ask.

### 2. Two bullets, never three
Three bullets always has a weakest one. Cut it. Pick the two strongest angles and make them sharper.

### 3. Bullet headers are nouns, not verbs
**Measurement.** **Gap.** **Business impact.** Single-noun headers scan instantly. Don't write "We tackle the measurement problem" — write "Measurement." then explain.

### 4. Use a transformation stat, not a volume stat
"49% to 86% in 3 weeks" beats "1,400 artifacts" beats "60,000 individuals." Always reach for a *change over time* stat. If you don't have one for the specific reader, lead with logos and use volume as backup. See stat hierarchy in facts.md.

### 5. Mirror their exact language
Highest-conversion pattern in the dataset. Patrick's email used his words ("interim signals of impact," "tracking through workflow"). Charles's used his framing ("the 70% problem"). Both booked meetings. When reading their public writing, copy 2-3 phrases verbatim. They will recognize themselves.

### 6. End with a spiky claim, not a soft ask
Patrick email's killer line: "The artifacts are the closest thing I've seen to tracking people through their workflow after upskilling — which you flagged as the hardest measurement problem." Spiky (you cracked their hard problem) but credited (you're acknowledging they named it). Soft asks get ignored; spiky claims get replies.

### 7. The P.S. is sacred personal-only territory
Never put business in the P.S. It's where you signal "I see you as a person." Use the recognition loop patterns from facts.md: same place, same network, same school, same career path, same essay/post. 3 numbered items max. 2 is fine. 1 is fine if it's strong.

### 8. Active engagements signal momentum
"We're kicking off a new cohort for legal and risk teams shortly" beats "we just finished a pilot." Active beats past. If there's no active thing, mention an upcoming launch, a paper in draft, an in-progress crosswalk. Future tense + "shortly" creates "I should reply now" energy.

### 9. Sign off with a place
"Best from Atlanta, David Fu" does work that "Best, David" can't. Humanizes. Adds a recognition vector. Distinctive enough to remember.

## The 4-Step Research Process

Time-box to 20-30 min per person.

### Step 1: LinkedIn deep-read (5 min)
- Recent posts (last 60 days, originals only — not reposts)
- Profile bio + headline
- Activity tab — what are they commenting on?
- Look for: spiky POVs, recent announcements, language they use repeatedly

**For email finding:** Google "[Name] [Company] email" + RocketReach + org email format inference. Never guess blindly.

### Step 2: Their company / public work (10 min)
- Their org's most recent published thinking on the topic
- Any podcasts they've appeared on (transcripts or notes)
- Their session title at the conference — *this is often the highest-signal source*
- Key phrases they use in their own writing — copy 2-3 verbatim

### Step 3: Find the spiky angle (5 min)
Ask: "What's a thing this person believes that I can either (a) confirm with proof they don't have, or (b) extend into territory they haven't explored yet?"

Pattern: **find the problem they named, claim you've cracked it, credit them for naming it.**

- Charles: wrote about 3 pitfalls of AI adoption → you have a daily-practice mechanism that addresses all 3.
- Patrick: wrote that workflow-tracking was the hardest measurement problem → you have 1,400+ artifacts that do exactly that.
- Drew: AI Fluency Index says 13 of 24 behaviors are unobservable → your AIQ Reflection assessment captures exactly those.

### Step 4: Find the personal recognition point (5 min)
Cross-reference facts.md against their LinkedIn / bio:
- Where did they live? Where have you lived?
- Where did they go to school?
- What's their network? Same circles?
- What's their career path? Does it parallel yours?
- Have they written something specific you can credit?

If you can't find ANY recognition point, the P.S. becomes a single warm line. Don't fake it.

### Step 5: Draft → Cut → Send
- Draft to ~280 words
- Cut to ~210 words
- Read aloud once
- Send

## Subject Line Patterns That Worked

| Type | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Their language + your stat | "the AI-native talent stack — 49% to 86% in 3 weeks" | Mirrors session title + drops proof |
| Their problem + your claim | "the 70% problem — and 1,400 data points from your team" | Names their framing + signals proof inside |
| Their gap + your fill | "the artifact paradox — and the 13 behaviors you can't see in the chat window" | Their finding + your answer |
| Spiky two-word phrase | "polished slop" | Memorable, low effort to open |
| Direct peer framing | "comparing notes on the talent stack (BCG pilot inside)" | Soft but specific, parenthetical signals substance |

Avoid: generic openers ("quick intro," "exploring partnership," "love your work"), question subjects unless very specific, anything with "AI" alone.

## Voice Rules

- **Lowercase subject lines.** Always.
- **First name only, no "Hi" or "Hello."** Just "[Name]," then "David here."
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, parentheses, or line breaks instead.
- **No exclamation points** except in casual P.S. items where they're warm.
- **Never** "I hope this email finds you well" or any variation.
- **Never** "I wanted to reach out" — you ARE reaching out, just do it.
- **Never** "Just" as a softener ("just wanted to," "just checking in") — kills authority.
- **Never** "circling back," "touching base," "synergy," "alignment," "leverage" as a verb.
- **No curse words** in cold outreach.
- **Numbers in digits** not words (10+ always digits; under 10 is flexible).
- **Bold the proof, not the headers.** Bold the BCG pilot reference, the metric, the framework name. Don't bold "Measurement."
- **Parenthetical asides are fine.** David uses them naturally for color, humor, context. Don't strip them.

## Event-Based Outreach Overlay

When preparing for a conference, apply this cadence on top of whatever email type:

- **7-10 days before:** Email 1 (or single email). Mention the event. Low-friction ask.
- **3-5 days before:** LinkedIn DM. Shorter; reference the email or re-state the hook.
- **At the event:** In-person. Use the observations from the email as conversation starters.
- **1-3 days after:** Warm follow-up referencing the in-person conversation.
