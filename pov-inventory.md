# David's Spiky POV Inventory

*A catalog of repeatable points of view to pull from when writing outreach. Each POV has: the claim, the origin, the sharpest one-liner, when to use it, the proof to pair with it, and example uses.*

*Quality bar for inclusion: a claim you've made at least twice in different contexts and gotten a meaningful reaction.*

---

## How to Use This File

1. Pick ONE POV per email that matches the recipient's world (two max if directly connected).
2. Mirror it to their language — each POV has multiple framings.
3. Pair it with proof. Spiky without proof reads as opinion; spiky with proof reads as authority.
4. Credit the source if it's not yours — "BCG's own 10-20-70 research says..." builds credibility faster than asserting it yourself.
5. End with the one-liner, not the explanation. They remember the punchline.

See POV Combinations section at the bottom for which pairs work together.

## POV #1 — "The Interface Is Not the Skill"

**Claim:** Most AI training teaches the tool (clicks, prompts, product walkthroughs) when AI is fundamentally a cognitive skill. The gap is not what people click — it's how they think before they open the tool.

**Origin:** Max Haining's core argument; the foundational thesis for 100 School's pedagogy.

**One-liner:** *"The interface is not the skill. Teaching clicks means we've fundamentally misunderstood the technology."*

**Alternate framings:**
- "AI is a cognitive skill, not a tool skill."
- "Most AI training teaches what to click. The actual gap is how people think before they open the tool."
- "Prompting is a symptom; the underlying skill is structured thinking."
- "If you teach the interface, you're obsolete every time the UI changes. If you teach the cognitive skill, you compound."

**When to use:** L&D buyers comparing vendors; anyone evaluating AI curricula; anyone frustrated with AI training ROI; counterpoint to tool-vendor-led training (Copilot, Google AI training).

**Proof to pair:** AIQ framework structure (3 dimensions, 12 competencies, all behavioral, none tool-specific); BCG pilot 49% to 86% in 3 weeks; "tool-agnostic so it stays relevant as Claude overtakes GPT and OpenClaw emerges"

**Example use (Charles Westrin):** "We built our own competency framework and assessment that's tool agnostic, then used it to design our challenge-based, async learning experience."

**Who to send to:** CLOs, L&D leaders, AI capability program leaders, consulting firm capability builders, university AI training designers.

## POV #2 — "The 70% Problem"

**Claim:** BCG's own 10-20-70 research says 10% of AI transformation effort should go to algorithms, 20% to tech and data, 70% to people and processes. Most enterprises invert this and wonder why ROI never materializes.

**Origin:** BCG's published research (Charles Westrin and broader BCG AI thought leadership). Use their language back at them.

**One-liner:** *"BCG's own research says 70% of AI transformation is people and processes. Most companies spend 70% on the tools."*

**Alternate framings:**
- "The 70% problem: companies invest in tools; transformation lives in people and processes."
- "Tools are the 10%. Everyone's been sprinting at the wrong number."
- "74% of companies struggle to scale AI value. That's not a tools problem."

**When to use:** BCG-affiliated audiences; McKinsey, Bain, Accenture; enterprise AI transformation buyers; anyone who's published on AI ROI gaps.

**Proof to pair:** BCG's 74% stat (ai-at-work 2025 report); 49% to 86% in 3 weeks (you operate in the 70%); 1,400+ artifacts and Slack culture change.

**Example use (Charles Westrin subject line):** "the 70% problem — 49% to 86% in 3 weeks"

**Who to send to:** BCG, McKinsey, Bain, Accenture people; consulting firm capability builders; PE portfolio operators; CLOs at AI-tool-heavy orgs with no ROI.

## POV #3 — "The Artifact Paradox"

**Claim:** When AI produces polished, fluent-looking outputs, users become less critical, not more. Anthropic's own AI Fluency Index shows polished outputs reduce evaluation behaviors by 3-5pp. The better AI gets at sounding right, the worse humans get at checking it.

**Origin:** Anthropic AI Fluency Index report; AIQ "Evaluate / Own the Result" dimension is the structured response.

**One-liner:** *"The better AI gets at sounding right, the worse humans get at checking it. That's the paradox underneath polished slop."*

**Alternate framings:**
- "Polished slop: when fluent-sounding output stops getting questioned."
- "Anthropic's own data: polished outputs reduce evaluation behaviors by 3-5pp."
- "'Looks good to me' syndrome is the new occupational hazard."

**When to use:** Anthropic / OpenAI / AI lab audiences; anyone who's read the AI Fluency Index; L&D buyers worried about output quality; anyone building evaluation rubrics.

**Proof to pair:** AIQ three-layer assessment (Outcome / Process / Reflection); "polished slop" framing with Drew Bent; 1,400+ artifacts as the dataset of what evaluation looks like.

**Example use (Drew Bent subject line):** "polished slop"

**Who to send to:** Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind; critical thinking academics; assessment companies (ETS, Pearson, Workera).

## POV #4 — "The 13 Unobservable Behaviors"

**Claim:** Anthropic's 4D AI Fluency Framework has 24 behavioral indicators. They can only observe 11 in actual chat data. The other 13 — including disclosure decisions, ethical judgment, consequences of sharing AI output — are the ones that matter most for enterprise. They explicitly say they can't measure them. We can.

**Origin:** Anthropic's published framework (Rick Dakan + Joe Feller); AIQ Reflection assessment type is the structured response.

**One-liner:** *"Anthropic can see 11 of 24 behaviors. The other 13 — the ones that actually matter for enterprise — happen outside the chat window."*

**Alternate framings:**
- "13 behaviors you can't see in the chat window — and why that's the real measurement problem."
- "Observation is not assessment. Anthropic published exactly that limitation; we built around it."

**When to use:** Anthropic team specifically (it's their framework); anyone working on AI assessment or fluency measurement; EU AI Act / compliance audiences.

**Proof to pair:** AIQ Reflection assessment type; 7-of-11 observable behaviors mapping to INSTRUCT only; "made the unobservable observable through structured reflection in daily challenges"

**Example use (Drew Bent follow-up):** "The 13 unobservable behaviors are arguably the most consequential — and you flagged that you can't track them in conversation. Our assessment uses three measurement types specifically to capture what happens outside the chat window."

**Who to send to:** Anthropic, OpenAI research, AI fluency academics, assessment companies, EU AI Act compliance audiences.

## POV #5 — "Iteration Is Trainable"

**Claim:** The most valuable AI skill isn't prompting — it's iteration. The ability to improve an AI output through a series of structured refinements is a learnable behavior, not a fixed trait. Most AI training ignores it entirely.

**When to use:** Curriculum designers; AI literacy researchers; enterprise L&D with advanced users who've plateaued.

**Proof to pair:** AIQ competency mapping; daily challenge design that builds iteration habits over 15 days.

## POV #6 — "The 30% Problem"

**Claim:** Most AI training focuses on the 70% of tasks AI can already do decently. The 30% — judgment, ethics, evaluation, context — is where humans still have to carry the load. But that's also the 30% most training programs don't build.

**When to use:** Pair with POV #5 (Iteration Is Trainable) for curriculum designers.

## POV #7 — "Tools-First Repeats with Agents"

**Claim:** We're about to repeat the same tools-first mistake with agents that we made with ChatGPT. Individual productivity gains, no team or org ROI, because nobody invested in people and processes first. AI-native teams are already putting agents in their pitch decks — the gap is widening.

**Origin:** Observed pattern from client work; used in Charles Westrin follow-up and general enterprise outreach.

**One-liner:** *"We made the tools-first mistake with ChatGPT. We're making it again with agents."*

**Alternate framings:**
- "Without deep investment in people, the agent era repeats the ChatGPT pattern: individual wins, no org ROI."
- "AI-native teams are putting OpenClaw in their pitch decks as a capability signal. Tool-heavy orgs are still stuck on adoption."

**When to use:** PE/VC; AI-native founders; enterprise CLOs who saw ChatGPT ROI disappoint; anyone running or evaluating AI capability programs.

**Proof to pair:** BCG pilot as the people-first counter-example; agent-era framing from industry; 80/20 flip stat.

## POV #8 — "Usage Is Not Capability"

**Claim:** High AI usage metrics mask capability gaps. 97% daily usage at Zapier is impressive — but token usage can be a golden pattern to multiply or an anti-pattern to coach out of. Usage data can't tell you which. Capability measurement can.

**Origin:** Brandon Sammut's own framing from MSN interview. Mirror their language.

**One-liner:** *"Usage is the floor, not the ceiling. The metric that matters is whether capability is growing underneath the usage curve."*

**Alternate framings:**
- "Token usage is a signal. Capability is the substance."
- "We went from 49% to 86% AI power users in 3 weeks — not by measuring usage, but by measuring behavior change."

**When to use:** L&D buyers; CHROs with usage dashboards they can't interpret; anyone who's used "adoption" as a success metric; product people measuring engagement.

**Proof to pair:** BCG pilot transformation stat; the three-assessment-type framework as the measurement layer; Brandon Sammut's own golden-pattern framing.

## POV #9 — "The Imagination Gap"

**Claim:** The biggest barrier to AI adoption isn't access or skill — it's the inability to imagine what AI could do for your specific work. Until someone can see AI as a thinking partner (not a search engine), the adoption ceiling stays low.

**Origin:** Observed pattern in BCG pilot — the leading indicator of power-user shift was when participants started applying AI to problems nobody had suggested.

**One-liner:** *"The imagination gap closes before the skills gap does. That's the leading indicator."*

**Alternate framings:**
- "The barrier isn't the tool — it's whether someone can see themselves using it."
- "In our BCG pilot, the shift from 49% to 86% was led by imagination, not prompting."

**When to use:** Shad Ahmed / economic mobility audiences; career transition programs; workforce development orgs; anyone focused on access and equity in AI adoption.

## POV #10 — "Human-First, Not Human-Versus"

**Claim:** The right frame isn't "AI replaces humans" or "AI assists humans" — it's that AI capability building is fundamentally human development. The same skills that make someone great at working with AI (structured thinking, judgment, iteration, evaluation) are the skills that make someone great at their job.

**When to use:** Mission-driven audiences; foundations; workforce development; anyone in the "AI will take our jobs" debate.

**Claim:** Most enterprises spend 80% of AI budget on tools and 20% on people. The companies getting AI ROI flipped it.

**Origin:** Yours. Variation of POV #2 (70% Problem) for non-BCG audiences.

**One-liner:** *"The companies getting AI ROI flipped the 80/20: 80% in people, 20% in tools."*

**When to use:** CLO/L&D buyer conversations; procurement/budget conversations; PE portfolio conversations; pair with POV #2 for BCG audiences, or use this alone for everyone else. Don't use both in the same email.

## POV #12 — "Survive the Adolescence"

**Claim:** AI is in its adolescence — powerful, awkward, unpredictable, occasionally dangerous. The next 5 years are about helping organizations survive the adolescence: building human judgment, governance, and adaptive capacity to navigate a technology that's still learning who it is.

**Origin:** Riffing on Amodei's "adolescence of technology" framing.

**One-liner:** *"AI is in its adolescence. The next 5 years are about helping organizations survive it."*

**When to use:** Risk / governance / compliance audiences; EU AI Act conversations; foundation / philanthropy; lofty-vision conversations with senior executives.

## POV #13 — "Two Futures, Same Technology, Different Choices"

**Claim:** The same AI technology can produce two completely different futures (Marshall Brain's "Manna": terrafoam dystopia or the Australia Project). The technology is neutral. The choices about deployment, who benefits, and how people are trained determine which future we get.

**One-liner:** *"Two futures, same technology, different choices. The choices are what we work on."*

**When to use:** Foundation / philanthropy / mission-driven audiences; lofty-vision conversations; counterpoint to deterministic AI narratives.

**Proof to pair:** Streetlight Schools experience; Reach Capital Scout role; 100 School centaur organization vision; JFF GenAI publication.

## 100 School Specific POVs (for Max / 100 School outreach contexts)

*These are additional angles specifically for outreach on behalf of 100 School or in the context of positioning 15 Days of AI.*

- **Daily practice beats courses.** A 15-minute daily challenge for 15 days changes habits; a 4-hour course completion certificate does not.
- **Behavior change is the product.** The artifact (a thing you actually made at work using AI) is the proof of capability, not the completion certificate.
- **Collaborative sharing multiplies impact.** The Slack momentum we observed in the BCG pilot — colleagues sharing outputs, commenting, building on each other's work — is a measurable culture indicator that pure usage data misses.
- **The centaur organization.** 100 School's vision: a future where every worker operates as a centaur — part human, part AI — and organizations are designed for that capability, not retrofitted.
